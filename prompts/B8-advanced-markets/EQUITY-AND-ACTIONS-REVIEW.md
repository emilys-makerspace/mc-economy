# Approved player-corporation equity and actions

The user approved these B8 game rules on 2026-11-17. They extend the confirmed private-share, optional-listing and one-way governance scope. See [the corporation expansion](CORPORATIONS-EXPANSION.md).

| Decision | Approved rule |
| --- | --- |
| Share classes | A corporation may name multiple classes. Each class starts with one vote per share or zero votes per share. All initial classes have equal economic rights per share to declared dividends and residual assets after creditors. Treasury shares carry neither votes nor dividends. Give each affected class a separate protective vote on amendments that weaken its rights or force it to exchange its shares, including a class with zero general votes. Publish class terms and current governance mode before a subscription or transfer. |
| Issuance and dilution | Set an explicit authorized share count per class. Offer a new cash issue first to existing holders of that class in proportion to their holdings, for 24 real hours at the same price and terms. Use whole-share allocations with largest remainders and a stable tie-break for the odd shares. After the window, offer unsubscribed shares on the disclosed terms. A lower price or better terms require a new offer. Founders and related parties participate on the same terms as everyone else in the class. Creating a new voting class after outside investment, increasing a class's voting power or changing its economic priority requires the protective vote of each adversely affected class. |
| Founder mode and transition | The founder authorizes ordinary actions initially, subject to the share-class protections and issuance rules above. Give all holders 24 real hours' notice before the one-way transition takes effect. Use the published class voting rights from that point. Changes to class rights packaged with the transition still need the affected classes' approval. Retain existing obligations and reconcile founder, manager and integration grants at effectiveness. |
| Voting mode | For routine actions such as a funded dividend or a new issue within the authorized share count, require participation by at least 50% of eligible voting power and more yes than no votes. Abstentions count toward participation. For increasing authorized shares, changing class rights, a reverse split, voluntary delisting or merging, require yes votes from at least two-thirds of **all** eligible voting power. Also require yes from two-thirds of the eligible shares in each adversely affected class, counted one vote per share for that class's protective vote. Use a 48-real-hour voting window and a settled holdings snapshot fixed when voting opens. Commit only the approved terms. Changed consideration, successor rights or other material terms require a fresh proposal and approval. |
| Dividends | Declare a flat amount per eligible share in one currency from fully reserved corporate cash. Announce the amount and UTC entitlement cutoff at least 24 real hours ahead. The same cutoff sets the record and ex-dividend boundary. Apply it before trades at that instant, pay the holders of settled shares immediately before it and publish payments against that snapshot. Freeze or account for supply changes when sizing the reserve. If the company cannot fund the declaration, leave it as a draft. On a loaned share, the issuer pays the eligible holder under the custody snapshot and the borrower separately owes the lender the full dividend equivalent under B7. |
| Splits | Use the same exact ratio across the issuer's classes initially, with one effective boundary across its listings. This preserves relative economic and voting interests before fractional settlement. Preserve total cost basis across whole shares and any fractions. Cancel affected unfilled orders and triggers at effectiveness so owners can review and resubmit. Prior fills and raw prices remain in history. Record any fractions as exact claims with funded cash settlement at a disclosed, independently supported value. If private shares lack that value, require an exact whole-share outcome or affected holders' explicit consent to a funded settlement price. Block publication when the action cannot preserve outstanding return, pledge or conversion claims under an accepted treatment. |
| Mergers and delisting | Start with one named surviving company. Both companies approve the identical package showing exchange ratios by class, any funded cash consideration, successor rights, debts, account consolidation and the effective UTC instant. Both approvals are required even when one founder owns both. For an interested deal, also require two-thirds of eligible disinterested shares **in each affected company with outside holders**, counted one vote per share. For the first release, require individual consent to a full cash-out of that holder's stake. Require voting mode for a cross-player survivor and preserve voting mode if either company already uses it. A same-owner survivor may retain founder control when both companies are still in founder mode. Retain old company and account records with successor links. Keep the survivor within its four-account bank limit. A voluntary delisting ends exchange execution and leaves privately held shares and existing obligations in place. |

## How the rules play out

- **Dilution.** A player owns 30 of 100 shares in a class. When the company offers 20 more shares of that class, the player gets the first chance to buy six on the same terms. Buying all six preserves 30% ownership. Passing leaves the player with 25% if all 20 sell.
- **Protective votes.** An exchange of an existing non-voting class requires that class's protective approval if the merger forces the exchange. Its holders still have zero votes on ordinary company business.
- **Reverse split.** Seven shares in a 1-for-3 split produce two whole shares and a one-third claim. Allocate basis between the whole shares and the fraction. Use the approved funded settlement or keep the action pending if it cannot be completed fairly.
- **Cross-player merger.** The two founders can negotiate that one old share becomes two shares of the survivor. Each company approves the exact deal under its own governance, and the survivor's voting terms take effect at closing. Any incompatible bank, collateral or share-return claim must be resolved before execution.
