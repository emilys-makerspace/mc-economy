## Concrete accounting and risk outcomes

A pool with 100 units locates 60, then sells 20 short and cancels 40 unfilled: 80 units are unloaned, 20 are a real return obligation. Cover 8 and return 8 leaves 12 outstanding; future fees apply only to 12. Do not charge borrowing on the cancelled 40. Onward lot lineage, public book anonymity and exact settlement compatibility must survive these operations.

A standalone short initially worth CAD 1,000 produces CAD 1,000 locked proceeds before fees and needs at least CAD 500 additional eligible own collateral for the 150% requirement. Costs/haircuts/minimum account equity can require more. If replacement value rises to CAD1, 200 while supporting collateral stays CAD 1,500, coverage is 125%, below 130% maintenance, opening a call. Cure to 150% requires total collateral CAD 1,800 (plus fees) or an actual risk-reducing cover. The account equity/combined exposure/leverage conditions still apply. Proceeds do not make the borrower CAD 1,000 richer: they offset a real obligation valued once.

At u=0 the borrow APR is 2%; at u=0.5 it is 6.5%; at u=1 it is 20%, each under the accelerated financial calendar. The curve is an approved game scarcity policy, not a real stock-lending rate quote or earnings guarantee. Only actually collected fees fund the 80/20 payout. A lender's claimed income cannot be credited from invented cash when a borrower is insolvent.

A recall for 20 with only 7 actual returned after buy-in leaves 13 outstanding and exact residual cash/collateral/risk. If no compatible sellers or funds exist, do not report a successful return, convert it to arbitrary cash or close the lending agreement. Pending fees/claims and truthful service status remain until accepted fulfillment/reconciliation.
