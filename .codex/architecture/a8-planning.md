## Confirmed scope

The user selected a broader macroeconomy driving both prime and FX, driven only by player/server economic activity, with a separate prime rate for each currency. This expands the previous prime-only downstream planning boundary. It does not approve a particular formula or implementation.

The user approved four activity inputs: completed merchant sales, borrowing/repayment, completed currency exchanges and operator-created money. Merchant spending subtracts refunds; credit distinguishes principal from interest and tracks funded capacity; FX records both sides; operator issuance is separately identifiable. Ordinary account transfers, cash deposits and withdrawals move existing funds rather than count as new spending or money creation. Exact attribution, normalization and rate effects remain design decisions.

The user also requested an overhaul of villager trading to use money instead of item currency/barter. Include this gameplay feature in A8 planning and its merchant-activity source contract under the approved policies below. This concerns villager goods commerce, separate from future B4 financial-market trading. A7 bankers retain their existing no-trading role unless explicitly changed.

Approved villager funding: unlimited purchasing funds. Do not require a finite village/villager treasury or prior customer spending to pay for an otherwise valid purchase from a player. Record newly created villager payouts with explicit issuance provenance, separate from operator issuance, while also recording the merchant sale. This extends the money-creation input to identify villager issuance as well as operator issuance, rather than adding an unrelated fifth driver. Existing finite bank-funding rules remain unchanged. Approved stock/rate caps appear below; normalization, circular-trade attribution and checked transaction bounds are specified in technical revision 1. Unlimited purchasing is not a stability guarantee.

Approved destination of player payments: remove money paid to villagers from circulation rather than retaining it in a treasury. Track villager issuance and retirement separately by currency, with their net monetary effect available to the simulation. Preserve the completed goods-sale signal independently; gross sales are not net money creation. Both cash and account settlement must record retirement through authoritative journal/custody contracts so the same payment cannot be spent again. Define exact posting/cash lifecycle and refund/reversal treatment before implementation. This retirement rule does not apply to ordinary player-owned merchants or bank receipts.

Approved villager pricing model: supply and demand rather than fixed prices. Player purchases increase demand/prices and player sales increase supply/reduce offered payouts. Approved response, bounds, spread, reputation and recovery appear below; technical revision 1 specifies exact arithmetic and item identities for feasibility review. A9 reference valuation is not implicitly selected as the A8 price source.

Approved market scope: village-wide pricing, with independent wandering traders. Traders in the same village share per-item supply/demand pricing state; each wandering trader has its own market state. This does not yet imply pooled physical inventory, identical profession offers or shared reputation discounts. Define stable market/item identities, existing/relocated trader membership, persistence and wandering-trader lifetime before implementation.

Approved price adjustments: supply and demand plus reputation. Apply the relevant player's reputation/discount adjustment on top of the shared market price, rather than changing village-wide demand state for that player. The accepted recommendation includes bounds against free purchases and profitable direct buy/sell loops. Define eligible reputation/effect sources, adjustment order, limits, buy/sell spread, minor-unit rounding and authoritative quote revalidation before implementation; the model choice does not approve numeric parameters or add reputation mechanics to wandering traders where no source exists.

Approved price recovery: prices gradually recover toward their base when trading slows, while the market is loaded and the server is running. Halve the remaining supply/demand price deviation every 24,000 eligible ticks (about 20 minutes at 20 ticks/second). Without further trades, a 40% premium becomes 20%, then 10%, then 5%; apply the same convergence toward base to below-base deviations. Restocking does not reset prices; sleep and offline time do not accelerate recovery. Define exact discrete/continuous arithmetic and residual rounding, precise village-market loaded eligibility (without counting multiple loaded traders as multiple clocks), wandering-trader eligibility and durable progress before implementation. This is recovery of local market pricing, not authorization for autonomous prime/FX drift. Unloaded time earns no recovery progress.

Approved supply/demand price bounds: 50%–200% of the base price, before reputation adjustments and the buy/sell spread. These bounds constrain the market multiplier, not a promise that every final player quote lies in that range. Define final positive-price safeguards, discount limits, spread and minor-unit rounding separately.

Approved trade sensitivity: move the market multiplier by one percentage point per standard trade quantity, upward when players buy and downward when players sell. Bulk quantities contribute proportionally; splitting a bulk trade into separate clicks must confer no advantage. Starting from 100%, ten standard purchases reach 110% before recovery and within the approved bounds. Define a stable per-item standard quantity shared across offers, component-sensitive identity, partial-quantity arithmetic and progressive batch quoting/settlement so differently sized offers or batches cannot evade this rule. Exact quantity normalization is implementation-contract work; this is a percentage-point change to the base multiplier, not 1% compounded growth per click.

Approved minimum buy/sell spread: after reputation adjustments, traders pay players at most 80% of what they charge for equivalent goods. Preserve larger existing gaps rather than increasing purchase offers to 80%. This is a cap relative to the selling price (a minimum 20% gap), not a symmetric 20% markup/markdown. Define equivalent component/ingredient quantities, cross-offer comparison within a village market, progressive batch application and conservative minor-unit rounding before implementation. Do not fabricate missing catalog offers merely to establish a comparison; define a canonical comparison price when only one direction is offered. Preserve positive-price and direct-loop safeguards under the final rounding rules.

Approved reputation limit: cap the combined reputation/discount adjustment at 25% in either direction relative to the supply/demand quote. Good reputation improves player prices by at most 25%; bad reputation worsens them by at most 25%. Apply the minimum buy/sell spread afterward, so a favorable reputation cannot override its payout cap. Freeze exact eligible sources and signed purchase/payout formulas in the shared contract; do not compound separate reputation sources beyond the combined cap.

Approved rate cadence: prime and FX both update once per existing UTC financial day, every 1,200,000ms (20 real minutes). The user clarified “in-game day” to mean 20 real minutes, not daylight boundaries or running ticks. Sleep and time commands do not accelerate updates; downtime creates ordinary retained catch-up. This supersedes the proposed split between 20-minute prime and 24-hour FX updates as the desired A8 behavior. Freeze alignment, activation, input cutoff/publication lag and historical catch-up semantics in the shared contract. The existing daily-UTC FX implementation remains authoritative until a separately designed prospective handover; do not mutate its stored cadence/signatures to satisfy the new schedule.

Approved no-activity rate behavior: prime and FX remain unchanged once prior activity has aged out of the approved seven-day window. No automatic drift toward initial values, synthetic cycles or random shocks. Technical revision 1 specifies retained-flow contribution. Complete zero-activity coverage is valid input; missing/corrupt observations are not equivalent to zero. This rate policy does not change the separately approved loaded-tick recovery of local goods prices.

Approved prime response directions, per currency: stronger spending relative to its recent norm pushes prime upward; greater borrowing relative to funded lending capacity pushes upward; principal repayments easing credit pressure push downward; net money creation pushes upward and net money removal pushes downward. FX buying/selling primarily affects exchange rates rather than directly changing prime. Normalize the signals and cap each update so one large transaction cannot cause an extreme jump. Exact observation baselines, flow-versus-stock treatment, weights, cancellation/zero-activity behavior, floors/ceilings and numeric step limits remain to be frozen. These are the selected gameplay rules, not permission to change captured fixed-loan terms or manufacture bank funding.

Approved FX response directions: net buying of a currency strengthens it, net selling weakens it, net money creation in that currency weakens it and net money removal strengthens it. Domestic sales/borrowing affect FX through actual money creation/removal or currency exchanges rather than an additional direct volume signal. Higher prime alone must not invent investor demand. Compute changes relative between currencies with normalized inputs and capped movement per update; retain the permanent-reference convention. Freeze common-value flow attribution, opposing-signal weights, reference normalization, step limits and cumulative bounds before implementation.

Approved activity window: use the last seven completed financial days (8,400,000ms, 2 hours 20 minutes real time) for each 20-minute rate update. This is a rolling window; after seven financial days without activity, the activity window is empty and rates hold unchanged. Freeze equal/weighted aggregation, historical-activity contribution across repeated updates, spending-comparison baseline, startup partial-window coverage and exact input/publication lag before implementation. Existing credit balances alone must not sustain autonomous rate drift once the approved activity window is empty.

Approved initial signal weights: prime uses equal weights for normalized spending, credit pressure and net money creation/removal (one third each). FX uses equal weights for normalized net exchange demand and net money creation/removal (one half each, with the approved response signs). Normalize each signal before weighting, rather than combining raw monetary totals. Keep weights configurable through immutable future policy revisions. Define exact normalization scales, neutral/baseline treatment, zero denominators, saturation and precision in the shared contract; temporal weights across the seven-day window are a separate implementation detail.

Approved loan-pricing selection: ordinary bank loans use the prime rate of the loan's currency; subprime retains its existing separate pricing rules. Existing fixed loans retain captured prime/premiums and APR. Carry the currency-specific policy identity through quote, application/origination, public API and provenance checks without changing historical contract meaning.

Approved per-update rate caps: prime moves by at most ±0.10 percentage points per currency per 20-minute update (for example, 6.00% to 6.10%). Any FX currency pair moves by at most ±0.5% per update. Smaller normalized signals may produce smaller movements. Cap the FX update coherently across the complete rate vector so cross-rates remain consistent; per-reference-currency caps alone do not establish the approved pairwise cap. Technical revision 1 specifies rational/decimal scaling, reciprocal-direction checks and conservative rounding. Overall prime and cumulative FX ranges are approved below.

Approved overall prime range: 0%–25% APR per currency, in addition to the per-update movement cap. This bounds prime, not the final loan APR after separately captured product/score premiums. Define prospective initialization and handling of pre-A8 global policies outside the new range without rewriting historical policies or fixed contracts.

Approved cumulative FX range: each currency pair remains within 50%–200% of its rate when A8 activates, alongside the ±0.5% per-update pairwise cap. Persist activation anchors; reloads do not reset them. Apply bounds coherently across the rate vector and its pairwise ratios, preserving the permanent reference at 1. Define exact anchor capture, rounding, currency availability, future currency additions and compatibility with operator overrides before implementation; do not silently re-anchor on server-denomination changes or normalize bounds independently into inconsistent cross-rates.

Approved simulation startup: initialize each currency's prime from the current configured global prime and FX from the latest committed publication. Observe activity prospectively; do not invent pre-A8 history or fill unobserved days with zero. Begin updates once a complete activity day is available, growing the rolling window until seven completed days are available. Retain coverage explicitly during warm-up and preserve existing fixed-loan terms. Freeze durable activation identity/boundary, publication lag, partial activation-day treatment, baseline normalization during warm-up, pre-existing future policies and handling of a legacy global prime outside 0%–25% before implementation. Startup approval does not authorize rewriting existing financial outcomes or bypassing the historical FX handover.

Approved administrator controls: pause automatic rate changes while activity tracking continues; set manual rates at a future financial-day boundary within the agreed limits; resume automatic updates explicitly using the current rolling activity window. Preserve historical rates and saved FX activation anchors. Freeze operator authorization, canonical operation identity, future-boundary conflicts, coherent full-vector FX editing and durable pause/resume state before implementation. A pause must produce explicit no-change processing rather than deleting scheduled work, resetting time or replaying paused rate movements on resume. It does not suspend ordinary banking, stock mechanics or activity capture.

Approved goods availability: limited goods for sale with restocking. Sale stock belongs to each individual trader, not a shared village stockpile; buying out one trader does not consume another trader's stock. Village traders still share per-item supply/demand prices. Buying can exhaust an offer despite unlimited merchant purchasing funds. Define per-offer stock quantities before implementation.

Approved restocking: village traders refill sale stock after 10,000 loaded, working ticks with access to their workstation. Wandering traders arrive with limited stock and do not restock. Restocking preserves supply/demand prices. Sleeping, unloading and server downtime grant no extra restocks. Define precise working/workstation eligibility, durable timer progress and restart handling before implementation; game-time jumps must not substitute for eligible running ticks. Approved offer catalogs: retain existing profession-specific trade catalogs and leveling rather than redesigning which goods each profession buys/sells; replace emerald payments and payouts with money. ## Causal model — technical contract prepared

1. Capture approved economic events atomically with their authoritative financial commits. Classify each event once by currency, effective time, source category and immutable journal/operation identity. Maintain bounded indexed aggregates rather than rescanning history for each publication.
2. Close immutable activity windows at financial-day boundaries. Record input frontier, policy version, coverage/availability and per-currency totals. Preserve prior snapshots and source semantics for backlog/recovery.
3. Derive per-currency indicators and prime from completed windows. Settle the exact publication lag before coding: an operator's future-date validator cannot be reused with a fake wall clock for historical catch-up. A trusted scheduler path needs event-time validation and atomic outcome/publication state.
4. Let the new financial-day FX adapter consume only immutable activity/model snapshots completed before its financial-day publication boundary, with an explicit lag. Record exactly which snapshot and model version supplied each currency's rate. Design prospective retirement/handover of the historical daily publisher without changing its persisted cadence/signature, duplicate publications or a same-instant cycle. A new algorithm version under the old fixed definition alone cannot change its cadence; inspect publication-day uniqueness/readers and define a compatible intraday publication identity/index contract.
5. Keep rate/indicator publication separate from money movement. New fixed loans capture the selected currency policy and APR at origination; existing contracts retain their captured values. Existing quotes revalidate the appropriate policy identity before settlement.

The permanent FX reference remains exactly 1. Relative rates must use one coherent reference convention, remain positive/representable, and reuse existing conversion rounding and immutable publication IDs. Decide how reference-currency activity affects relative rates through comparisons with other currencies. Define new activity-driven bounds and operator control without modifying historical `FxMath.advance` semantics.
